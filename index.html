The law

This is a disclosure requirement, not a preference

Colleges must “prominently display … the estimated costs for each course of all required course materials and fees directly related to those materials.”

Education Code § 66406.9, as amended by AB 607 (2023)

Federal law already required the ISBN and retail price of required and recommended textbooks, plus the cost of supplemental materials, to be disclosed in the course schedule at the time of registration.

20 U.S.C. § 1015b · Higher Education Opportunity Act

Where we are

Building the picture, then getting it in front of students

We are collecting more accurate and more complete course materials information than we have before, and more sections are reporting each term.

The next step is publishing it on the class schedule, so the cost information reaches students at the point where it helps them, which is when they register. We are working on that now.

What we need from you is the first half: complete, detailed, timely entries for every section.

The most common mistake

Zero cost does not mean no materials

Common issues

  • The book is free, so nothing gets reported
  • “No required materials” chosen because nothing is purchased
  • “This course uses OER” with no title, author, or link
  • No ISBN and no edition, which leaves nothing to verify the price against
  • Access codes, lab kits, and software left off

What the form needs

  • An OER course has required materials. They are free, not absent.
  • Every item listed separately, required ones marked required
  • Title, author, edition, ISBN, and link
  • Your best estimate of the cost. We verify with eCampus.
  • Enough detail that a student could find and buy it from your entry alone

All required materials

It is not only the textbook

AB 607 requires disclosure of all instructional materials associated with a course, plus fees directly related to them. The Chancellor's Office names the first three specifically. The list is not closed.

01

Textbooks

Digital or physical.

02

Devices

Calculators, and remote attendance platforms.

03

Software subscriptions

Including access codes bundled with a text.

04

Supplies

Lab kits, safety goggles, art materials, scrubs, tools, anything a student must buy.

05

Related fees

Fees directly tied to those materials.

A free textbook paired with a $60 access code is not a zero-cost course. If anything still costs money, the section is not ZTC.

CCCCO memo ESLEI 24-32 · Education Code § 66406.9

Also on you

Cost minimization is a stated duty

District policies “shall direct instructors to take reasonable steps to minimize the cost and ensure the necessity of instructional materials.”

Title 5, California Code of Regulations, § 59404(a)

Separately, an instructor who requires materials they authored, or from which they receive royalties, must disclose that to the institution.

Education Code § 66406.7, College Textbook Transparency Act
